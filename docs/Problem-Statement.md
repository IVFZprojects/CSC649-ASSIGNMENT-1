# Problem Statement

## A1. Problem Statement

Patients at small primary clinics in Malaysia cannot see how long they must wait before they see a doctor. Clinics still use paper tickets and call out names, so patients arrive early and sit in a crowded waiting room because they are afraid to miss their turn. This wastes their time, makes the clinic more crowded, and causes stress. A study at a Malaysian primary clinic found the average waiting time was about 41 minutes (Ahmad et al., 2017). Patients who do not know their wait time are more likely to stay inside the clinic, which makes crowding worse and lowers satisfaction.

## Evidence

| # | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | With manual queue numbers, patients must arrive early and wait in the clinic the whole time in case they miss their call. | Arnoldus & Suprihadi (2021) |
| E2 | Not knowing when they will be served causes stress and forces patients to stay in the waiting room. | Nasrudin et al. (2023) |
| E3 | Patients go through three stages: registration, consultation, and pharmacy. Current digital queue systems do not connect all three stages in one ticket. | Ahmad et al. (2017); Fauzi & Suhaimi (2025) |

## A2. Users and Scope

### Primary Users

| User | Description | Main Need |
|---|---|---|
| Patient | A person who visits the clinic as a walk-in or with an appointment | Know the waiting time and get an alert before their turn |
| Receptionist or nurse | Clinic staff who register patients and manage the queue | Manage the queue from one dashboard without calling names aloud |
| Doctor | The clinician who sees each patient | See who is next and the patient's current status |
| Pharmacy staff | Staff who prepare and hand out medicine | Know when a patient is ready to collect medicine |

### Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Interest |
|---|---|
| Clinic owner or admin | Wants a low-cost system that reduces crowding and improves clinic reputation |
| Ministry of Health Malaysia | Interested in improving primary care service quality |
| Course lecturer | Assesses the quality of the problem, requirements, and technology explanation |

### In Scope

- QR code check-in for walk-in patients
- Real-time queue position shown on the patient's phone
- Machine learning estimate of waiting time
- "Leave now" push notification when three patients are ahead
- Appointment booking by time slot
- One ticket that tracks the patient from registration to pharmacy
- Staff queue dashboard (web-based)

### Out of Scope

- Billing and payment
- Insurance or medical claim processing
- Connection to hospital systems or the Ministry of Health database
- Native mobile app (iOS or Android)
- Telemedicine or video consultation
- Full electronic medical records system
